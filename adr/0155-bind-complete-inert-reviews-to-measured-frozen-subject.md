# 0155. Bind complete inert reviews to a measured frozen subject

Status: Accepted

## Context

Source-built fixed-point preparation produces large, inert source profiles and
six selected execution recipes. A source regression or a source verifier's
`ready` result does not establish complete graph/physical review of those
programs. The rejected `9300895d…` review returned useful measurements but
explicitly lacked exhaustive nested, guard, closure and root coverage.

Earlier retained inspection programs also embedded a particular cohort's
paths, identity values, counts and rejection text. Reusing that text after a
source repair could mislabel old measurements as current evidence. Treating
ControllerSpec words in the outer controller's generic input-alias scope also
produces false diagnostics; actual generated template scopes are authoritative.

## Decision Drivers

- Bind every observation to the actual qualified source, runner and prepared data.
- Make incomplete static coverage a visible launch blocker, not sampled acceptance.
- Keep inert inspection separate from product execution and protected evidence.
- Distinguish source/program/NAR/physical identity from raw file and transport hashes.
- Preserve consumer-local scopes and exact source authority without guessed kinds.
- Retain complete counters with bounded diagnostic examples rather than duplicated payload dumps.

## Decision

The retained graph, template and physical inspectors accept explicit
`COHORT FROZEN_SOURCE REVIEW_SCRATCH SUBJECT_JSON` arguments. They reuse the
existing repository-owned review-subject field vocabulary. Supplied identities
are expected bindings to independently compare with actual files, plans,
reports and physical/source records, not observations to echo as success.

Graph and template inspection retain one integration owner and separate code
ownership with a fixed helper interface. Every selected recipe, static/nested
invocation and finite generated template must have measured total/covered
counts for its relevant reference, word, guard, source-authority and root
obligations. Known compiler data does not acquire path authority. Source or
ManifestSource kinds must come from admitted bindings and the frozen consumer;
unknown kinds remain explicit uncovered criteria.

Runtime-generated invocation bytes do not exist in inert data. Their complete
symbolic same-turn/same-slot producer, emitter, strict-decoder and routing
contract is inspected separately, while actual bytes, decode and execution
remain protected-run observations. Do not merge these future observations into
unknown static binding-kind counts or fabricate runtime products.

Physical inspection separately binds all seven materialized roles, four
program records and their actual bodies/bytes/chunks/NARs, original authenticated
sources and admitted frozen/prepared leaves under the actual capture policy.
Preauthor and augmented source trees are different identity classes.

Reports derive bounded verdicts from actual diagnostics and uncovered launch
criteria. The parent runs the integrated inspectors on the newly qualified and
regenerated subject and retains exact commands, tool/inspector/consumer hashes,
results and limitations. Old model output, old counts, partial success, file
availability or transport equality cannot grant launch authority.

## Consequences

- Mantle's source-built proof/operator workflow owns this mechanism. Its current
  consumer is the source-built fixed-point cold-launch gate; the durable
  contribution is a reusable explicit-subject inspection boundary rather than
  another one-cohort acceptance string.
- Implementations and their actual current-data runs are retained with the
  active change. No implementation or successful new-data execution is implied
  by this decision record itself.
- A code repair requires a new published/frozen source, isolated runner/canonical
  qualification and regenerated programs. Review identity and coverage must be
  measured again; no old ACCEPT or hardcoded rejection/count is carried over.
- Static review remains advisory evidence. It does not establish compiler
  correctness, protected execution, fixed-point equality, reproducibility,
  signed native-row acceptance or release eligibility.
